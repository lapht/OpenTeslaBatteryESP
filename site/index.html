Check your Tesla's battery health with a cheap ESP32.

Plug it into the car, join its WiFi with your phone, and read what the battery management system reports about your pack.

  • Listen-only. The CAN controller runs in listen-only mode: it never transmits, not even an acknowledge bit.
  • No app, no cloud, no account. The ESP serves the page itself over its own WiFi.
  • Cheap parts. An ESP32 board, a CAN transceiver and an OBD-II plug, roughly 15–30 € in total.

How it works

The battery management system already broadcasts its own capacity estimate on the car's CAN bus. The device just listens.

1 · Listen

Read the CAN bus

An SN65HVD230 transceiver feeds the ESP32's CAN controller, set to listen-only at 500 kbit/s.

2 · Decode

Compute health

It decodes the BMS frames for original and current pack energy and computes state of health and degradation.

3 · Show

Open it on your phone

Join the OpenTeslaBattery-XXXX WiFi (password teslabattery) and the page opens by itself, or go to http://tesla.lan/.

Flash the firmware

Connect the ESP32 with a USB cable and install the latest release. Works in Chrome or Edge on a computer.

From the browser

Checking for the latest release…

Your browser does not support Web Serial. Use Chrome or Edge on a computer, or the command line. Browser flashing needs a secure (HTTPS) page.

From the command line

Download the .bin for your board from the releases page, then:

pip install esptool
esptool.py --chip esp32 write_flash 0x0 openteslabattery-esp32dev.bin

To set your own WiFi password, build from source with OTB_AP_PASSWORD.

Wiring

OBD-II port pins 6 and 14 carry CAN at 500 kbit/s, according to Tesla's own bulletin. Details and the center console fallback in docs/wiring.md.

FAQ

Can it damage the car or change anything?

The CAN controller runs in listen-only mode: it does not transmit frames, acknowledge bits or error frames, and the firmware has no send function. It only reads what is already on the bus.

Is this Tesla's official battery health test?

No. It shows the BMS's own estimate of pack energy, which can drift a few percent with recent charging. The most stable reading comes after a charge to 100 % and some rest.

Does it work on LFP packs?

The values come from the BMS, so they should be there, but the estimate is less reliable on LFP chemistry. The reference car is NCA. LFP detection and a warning are planned.

Will it drain the 12 V battery?

On the OBD-II port, power is only on while the car is awake. If you power it from an always-on source, unplug it when you are done.

Which cars are supported?

Model 3 first (development car: Model 3 Long Range AWD 2021). Model Y should be similar and is planned for v1.0.
